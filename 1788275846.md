# Winemaking Mythos

Winemaking is one part knowledge and eight parts myth with a dash of luck for
good measure. Like it or not, wine is inevitable. So long as the few basic
steps are followed, it can be said that the way each producer makes their wine
is entirely up to them. The grapes can be squished, fermented, pressed,
sun-dried, cooked, or manipulated a million other ways and wine can and will be
derived from them. We all concoct some kind of story in our minds paired with a
logical explanation for each little thing we do and don't do. Really, it all
sounds very smart and like all myths, every believer is right (despite the fact
the myth cannot be verified) and every non-believer is horribly wrong (even if
they have verifiable evidence). That's just the way it goes. So some winemakers
do this and other winemakers do that and all are both correct and incorrect but
at the end of the day, everyone is drunk. Wine is fermented grape juice but
it's also dreams and memories and life and mythos. It's a whole world in a
bottle, it's love, it's a magical gift from God, or it could be, if that
meaning was put into it. It could also just be cheap, shallow and meaningless
plonk if that is the desire of the winemaker. Here's the thing though, the
nature of reality is such that the meaning ascribed to wine becomes manifest
and the wine takes on mythical form. This could be logically explained as the
winemaker's philosophy driving decision making and thereby affecting the wine
produced as a result, but, I believe it's so much more than that. The wine
affects the winemaker as much as the winemaker affects the wine. The winemaker
is an instrument through which the wine can express itself. That being said, an
instrument out of tune cannot join in the harmony of the orchestra of Nature.
This might just be me pushing my mythos onto you, the reader, however, wine
made by a sophisticated winemaker is only good for sophisticants and no good
for anyone else. Wine made by natural people can only truly be appreciated by
natural people. I would go so far as to say that wineries don't have customers,
they have fans and fandoms. Like a dog which resembles its owner or an owner
which resembles their dog, wine drinkers resemble their choice of wine. The
plonk drinkers look like the plonk they drink and the sophisticants have their
noses pointed to the sky. I don't know if the wine makes the drinkers (maybe
nefariously so) or if the drinkers make the wine. So much meaning is put into
wine and so much myth has been created that anyone looking to become an expert
on the global, multi-billion dollar industry surrounding it requires many years
of dedicated study. As more vintages are made and more wineries and winemakers
are born, the mythosverse of nonsense grows. I am of the opinion that this will
all implode soon and hopefully then, hopefully, there will only be one wine.
